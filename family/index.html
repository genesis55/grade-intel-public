Using public Wi-Fi safely (with a VPN)

Free Wi-Fi at libraries and coffee shops is open to anyone, so others on the same network can try to see or fake what you're doing. A VPN wraps your connection in encryption so they can't.

  1. Use your phone's hotspot when you can. It's safer than any public network.
  2. Make sure it's the real network. Ask staff for the exact Wi-Fi name. Fake networks often use a look-alike name, such as an extra "Free" at the end.
  3. Sign in to the Wi-Fi first, then turn on the VPN. Accept the network's sign-in page, open your VPN app and wait until it says Connected before you open anything else.
  4. Use a trusted VPN only. Use one your parents approve, such as Proton VPN (it has a free plan). Skip random free VPN apps; some sell your data. Turn on its kill switch so nothing leaks if the VPN drops.
  5. Don't put a VPN on a school device. MCPS laptops have their own protection and may not allow one; on a school device, stick to school sites and Canvas.
  6. Look for the padlock. Only type passwords on sites that start with https and show a padlock. If the browser warns that a site isn't secure or the certificate is wrong, close it; never click through.
  7. Turn off sharing. Set AirDrop to Contacts Only (or Off) and turn off file sharing while you're out.
  8. Forget the network when you leave. That stops your device from joining it, or a fake copy of it, automatically next time.
  9. Keep your device updated. Install system and browser updates; they fix the holes attackers use.
